\begin{abstract}
However, state-of-the-art recommender systems based on graphs deployed in web-based platforms are inherently vulnerable to two correlated phenomena: (1) susceptibility to adversarial shilling attacks and temporal review-bombing attacks, which are deliberate insertion of deceiving feedback to affect the exposure of the items, and (2) strong popularity bias of the catalog, since Pareto interaction distributions result in long-tail items being neglected. The existing approaches tackle either adversarial robustness or popularity debiasing separately and fail to account for both phenomena at the same time. In this paper, we introduce \textbf{RRFN-LLM-SUPER}, a complete solution framework that combines stochastic noise transition modeling, the \textbf{Multi-view Interaction Reliability Filter (MIRF)}, risk-aware Graph Contrastive Learning (GCL), and Pareto debiasing approach. We formulate rigorous mathematical foundations and prove that the Tikhonov-regularized noise inversion is bounded by $\|T^{\dagger}_\lambda\|_2 \le \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}$ avoiding operator singularity or divergence, and the risk-consistency of the asymptotic surrogate loss is $\mathcal{O}(\delta + 1/\sqrt{N})$ with confidence inversion bounds $\mathcal{O}(\delta/\sqrt{\lambda} + \sqrt{\lambda})$. MIRF learns continuous sample reliability weights $w_{ui} \in [0, 1]$ using synthesis of model prediction consistency, asynchronous LLM-based semantic review verification via thread-safe SQLite WAL caching mechanism, and BLAS vectorized burst scoring. The weights serve as modulating factors of InfoNCE contrastive losses, while ensuring purification of historical blueprints for personalized tail inclination ($\alpha_u$) assignment. Comprehensive empirical studies involving 5 independent random seeds and 3 benchmark domains (subgraph of MovieLens-1M, Amazon Reviews, and Yelp Academic) featuring several neural architectures (NeuMF, SimGCL, and SGL) reveal that RRFN-LLM-SUPER is able to detect attacks with an Average Precision of \textbf{0.6884} on Amazon and \textbf{0.6816} on Yelp ($3.9$--$4.6\times$ improvement compared to the performance of random edges), retain an Authentic Retention AP of \textbf{0.9989} (ROC-AUC = $\mathbf{0.7931 \pm 0.0038}$ on MovieLens-1M in case of 15\% bandwagon attack), provide statistically significant improvements in popularity calibration error (RMSE-PC = $\mathbf{0.1067 \pm 0.0056}$, $p = 0.0166$; MRMC = $\mathbf{0.1128 \pm 0.0034}$, $p = 0.0088$), eliminate attack-driven deceptive tail inflation (reduction of spam exposure STER@10 from 88\% to 72\%), increase true long-tail discovery ($g$-LTC@10 = $0.4202 \pm 0.0333$ vs. $0.3371 \pm 0.0693$, $p = 0.0397$ on Yelp), and produce statistically significant improvements in offline Monte Carlo bootstrap simulations (1,000 iterations). Complete source code, configurations, and evaluation logs are publicly accessible at \url{https://github.com/advaitvarhade/RRFN-LLM-SUPER}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Motivation}
\label{sec:intro}

\IEEEPARstart{W}{eb-scale} recommender systems work on bipartite user-item interaction graph $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{I}, \mathcal{E})$. However, real-world recommendation graphs suffer from two mutually orthogonal forms of deterioration: \textbf{adversarial data corruption} and \textbf{structural popularity bias}~\cite{wu2021robust,abdollahpouri2019popularity}.

First, adversaries plan large-scale injection attacks, including bandwagon shilling and temporal review bombing~\cite{si2022shilling,trustgrs2025}. In the bandwagon attacks, the automated bot accounts inject extremely high ratings for target items together with extremely high ratings for popular anchor items to mask their behavior. The standard Collaborative Filtering (CF) and Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) do not distinguish between different types of edges and hence the noise propagates through high-order message passing layers and misaligns user/item representations~\cite{he2020lightgcn,wang2019ngcf}.

Second, the interaction behavior of users is extremely concentrated according to Pareto distribution: approximately $20\%$ of the head items receive over $80\%$ of interactions~\cite{yang2022super,steck2018calibrated}. The standard objective functions minimize global empirical loss, and hence encourage the popular items while suppressing relevant niche items in the long tail.

\subsection{Grounding Key Concepts: SUP, RRFN, and MIRF}
To establish conceptual and terminological precision throughout this paper:

\textbf{1. What is SUP?} \textbf{SUP} (\textbf{SUPER}) is the abbreviation of \textit{Soft Blueprint Merging for Calibrated Long-Tail Recommendation}~\cite{yang2022super} in the context of literature. SUP considers the problem of catalog popularity skew through partitioning of items into Head model ($M_{\text{pop}}$) and Tail model ($M_{\text{tail}}$) as well as soft merging of candidate lists according to user tail inclination $\alpha_u$. Nonetheless, SUP inherently makes the assumption of a perfect, pristine interaction graph. Upon the presence of adversarial shilling or review bombing noise, SUP's interaction blueprints take in corrupted ratings causing substantial degradation of recommendation quality.

\textbf{2. What is RRFN?} \textbf{RRFN} is \textit{Robust Risk-consistent Fusion Network}. RRFN is a framework for modeling noise transitions that creates a $K \times K$ stochastic rating transition matrix $T_{ij} = P(\tilde{Y} = j \mid Y^* = i)$ from high-confidence anchor items, uses Tikhonov regularization to stabilize the inverse of the matrix, and minimizes the risk-consistent bounded forward loss function $\mathcal{L}_{\text{risk}}$.

\textbf{3. What is MIRF?} \textbf{MIRF} refers to \textit{Multi-view Interaction Reliability Filter}. MIRF is the unified tri-view ensemble framework consisting of the following views:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $R_{\text{RRFN}}$: Statistical consistency of model predictions based on regularized transition matrix $T$.
    \item $R_{\text{LLM}}$: Semantic LLM review-text validation~\cite{fan2023recommender} using asynchronous prompting queries and caching in the thread-safe SQLite WAL database.
    \item $R_{\text{bomb}}$: Vector-based temporal-polarity review-bombing burst identification using 2D BLAS matrix broadcasting.
\end{itemize}
The output of MIRF is a continuous sample reliability weight $w_{ui} = R_{\text{MIRF}}(u, i) \in [0, 1]$.

\textbf{4. Our Unified Approach (RRFN-LLM-SUPER):} We introduce the \textbf{RRFN-LLM-SUPER} architecture, which merges the noise reduction ability of RRFN and MIRF, the unsupervised learning of representation alignment in Graph Contrastive Learning (SimGCL / SGL) framework, and the bias correction capability of SUPER. By performing interaction blueprint filtering via MIRF reliability check and weighting the InfoNCE contrastive loss via sample weights, we achieve coordinated attack resilience and long-tail recovery simultaneously.

\subsection{Summary of Contributions}
The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Mathematical Noise Transition \& Operator Boundedness Proof}: We construct a stochastic noise transition matrix $T$ and prove (Theorem~\ref{thm:bound}) that Tikhonov regularization $(T^T T + \lambda I)^{-1} T^T$ is bounded in spectral operator norm by $\|T^{\dagger}_\lambda\|_2 \le \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}$, ensuring Lipschitz gradient stability and numerical safety. Further, we establish (Theorem~\ref{thm:consistency}) the forward surrogate risk consistency bound $\mathcal{O}(\delta/\sigma_0 + 1/\sqrt{N})$, converging asymptotically to clean Bayes risk, alongside regularized confidence inversion error bound $\mathcal{O}(\delta/\sqrt{\lambda} + \sqrt{\lambda})$.
    \item \textbf{Multi-view Interaction Reliability Filter (MIRF)}: We propose a MIRF ensemble layer including the model prediction divergence ($R_{\text{RRFN}}$), prompt-audited LLM semantic review verification ($R_{\text{LLM}}$) aided by a thread-safe SQLite WAL cache, and 2D BLAS vectorized temporal-polarity burst scoring ($R_{\text{bomb}}$), which provides a $500\times$ speedup ($4.95\,$ms on 42K interactions).
    \item \textbf{Risk-Aware Graph Contrastive Learning}: SimGCL (latent uniform noise) and SGL (structural dropout) backbone models are combined with a sample-reliability weighted InfoNCE contrastive loss to prevent poisoned interactions from poisoning graph embeddings.
    \item \textbf{Purer SUPER Blueprint Merging \& Attack-Aware LTC Metrics}: We present Genuine Long-Tail Coverage ($g$-LTC@K) and Spam Target Exposure Rate (STER@K), demonstrating that raw LTC inflation under attack corresponds to malicious spam injection and not actual discovery of genuine users, and develop an anchor-free blueprint engine that filters out any form of fabricated feedback.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Domain 5-Seeds Benchmarking and Statistical Analysis}: We benchmark RRFN-LLM-SUPER with 5 seeds independently in three domains (MovieLens-1M subgraph, Amazon reviews with SimGCL, and Yelp Academic with SGL), providing exact mean $\pm$ standard deviation, statistical significance with paired $t$-test, ablation study, and 1,000 samples Monte Carlo bootstrapping finance simulation ($p < 0.001$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work and Positioning}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Robust Collaborative Filtering \& Noise Denoising}
Prior robust collaborative filtering and generative recommendation techniques addressed filtering out shilling attacks or latent noise through heuristic clustering, variational inference~\cite{liang2018variational}, and threshold-based outlier detection~\cite{wu2021robust,si2022shilling}. Loss correction techniques in computer vision~\cite{patrini2017making} showed that transition matrices $T$ could theoretically asymptotically revert label manipulations. Recent recommender systems like T-CE~\cite{gao2022self} used transition matrices for implicit feedback. Nevertheless, such techniques rely on the assumption of uniformly randomly distributed noise and do not work in face of non-stationary coordinated review bombing characterized by temporal burst and polarity skews.

\subsection{Popularity Bias \& Long-Tail Calibration}
Research of popularity bias in recommender systems has employed causality graphs, interventions, propensity score weighting, and calibrated ranking~\cite{abdollahpouri2019popularity,steck2018calibrated,zheng2021disentangling}. Independent SUPER~\cite{yang2022super} technique suggested soft merging blueprints in order to compensate for user-specific long-tail quotas. Yet, prior debiasing research assumed only unbiased training interactions and, hence, fails to handle cases when an adversary introduces popular items in order to run a bandwagon attack.

\subsection{Graph Contrastive Learning \& Trust-GRS Positioning}
GCL algorithms including SGL~\cite{wu2021sgl}, SimGCL~\cite{yu2022simgcl}, and NCL~\cite{lin2022ncl} improve uniformity by means of data augmentation and neighborhood-enriched noise perturbation. The recent Trust-GRS~\cite{trustgrs2025} work studies trustworthy graph neural network models in the face of shilling attacks by contrastive learning, while other solutions including AutoDebias~\cite{chen2021autodebias} work to mitigate popularity bias in clean interaction graphs. Our solution significantly diverges from and goes beyond these baseline solutions in three basic ways:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Combined Denoising and Pareto Debiasing}: As opposed to the graph representation focus of Trust-GRS that does not solve catalog popularity bias problems and the clean interaction graphs of AutoDebias, RRFN-LLM-SUPER integrates robust learning of graph representation and Pareto partition calibration.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Modal LLM and Vectorized Burst Auditing}: In addition to the only graph topological contrastive views, our solution integrates the statistical rating transition matrix ($R_{\text{RRFN}}$), semantic text reviews auditing ($R_{\text{LLM}}$), and 2D BLAS burst polarities temporal auditing ($R_{\text{bomb}}$).
    \item \textbf{Mathematical Proof of Bounded Gradient}: We provide formal proof for the boundedness of non-singular Tikhonov gradients and excess risk asymptotic.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Theoretical Foundations and Mathematical Analysis}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Noise Transition Model and Anchor Assumptions}
The observed ratings $\tilde{Y} \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ are treated as a noisy observation of the underlying clean ratings $Y^* \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ under the conditional transition matrix $T \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times K}$:
\begin{equation}
    T_{ij} = P(\tilde{Y} = j \mid Y^* = i), \quad \sum_{j=1}^K T_{ij} = 1.
\end{equation}
Let us define $\mathbf{p}(u, i) \in \Delta^{K-1}$ as the distribution over clean rating classes predicted by our model, where $\Delta^{K-1}$ denotes the probability simplex. The distribution over observed noisy ratings takes the form:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf{p}}(u, i) = T^T \mathbf{p}(u, i).
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Anchor Separability and Prior Consistency]
\label{ass:anchor}
There exists for every rating class $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ a set of anchor items $\mathcal{A}_k \subset \mathcal{I}$ such that their rating is known to be $k$ with high precision:
\begin{equation}
    P(Y^* = k \mid i \in \mathcal{A}_k) \ge 1 - \epsilon_{\mathrm{anchor}},
\end{equation}
which allows us to estimate the transition prior between anchors $\hat{T}_{kj} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}_k|} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}_k} P(\tilde{r}_{ui} = j)$ with Frobenius error bound $\|\hat{T} - T^*\|_F \le \delta$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Bounded Logits and Non-Singular Transition Matrix Spectrum]
\label{ass:bounded}
The clean logits $\mathbf{z}(u, i) \in \mathbb{R}^K$ are bounded in norm, $\|\mathbf{z}\|_\infty \le C < \infty$. The true transition matrix $T^*$ is regular with its minimum singular value being $\sigma_{\min}(T^*) \ge \sigma_0 > 0$.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Theoretical Analysis of Tikhonov Noise Inversion}

\begin{definition}[Tikhonov-Regularized Pseudo-Inverse]
\label{def:tikhonov}
Given transition matrix $T \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times K}$ and damping factor $\lambda > 0$, the Tikhonov-regularized pseudo-inverse is given by:
\begin{equation}
    T^{\dagger}_{\lambda} = (T^T T + \lambda I)^{-1} T^T.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Spectral Operator Norm Bound and Gradient Stability]
\label{thm:bound}
For transition matrix $T \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times K}$ with singular value decomposition $T = U \Sigma V^T$ with singular values $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_K \ge 0$, for all $\lambda > 0$, the spectral operator norm of the Tikhonov-regularized pseudo-inverse is strictly bounded by:
\begin{equation}
    \|T^{\dagger}_{\lambda}\|_2 \le \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}.
\end{equation}
Furthermore, for the forward-corrected surrogate loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{risk}} = \ell_{\mathrm{CE}}(T^T \mathbf{p}(X), \tilde{\mathbf{y}})$ with $L_{\mathrm{loss}}$-Lipschitz cross-entropy, parameter gradient satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \|\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{risk}}\| \le L_{\mathrm{loss}} \|T\|_2 \|\nabla_\theta \mathbf{p}\| \le L_{\mathrm{loss}} \sqrt{K} \|\nabla_\theta \mathbf{p}\|.
\end{equation}
As $\|T\|_2 \le \sqrt{\|T\|_1 \|T\|_\infty} \le \sqrt{K}$ for any $K \times K$ row-stochastic transition matrix $T$ by Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem. Additionally, inverting predicted ratings for confidence estimation and sample reliability $R_{\text{RRFN}}$ through $T^{\dagger}_{\lambda}$ is strictly protected from gradient explosion and noise amplification via the strict bound $\|T^{\dagger}_{\lambda}\|_2 \le \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}$, regardless of the condition number $\kappa(T)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substituting $T = U \Sigma V^T$ into Definition~\ref{def:tikhonov}:
\begin{align}
    T^{\dagger}_{\lambda} &= (V \Sigma^T U^T U \Sigma V^T + \lambda I)^{-1} V \Sigma^T U^T \nonumber \\
    &= (V (\Sigma^2 + \lambda I) V^T)^{-1} V \Sigma^T U^T \nonumber \\
    &= V (\Sigma^2 + \lambda I)^{-1} \Sigma U^T.
\end{align}
The singular values of $T^{\dagger}_{\lambda}$ are given by the continuous scalar function $f(\sigma) = \frac{\sigma}{\sigma^2 + \lambda}$ for $\sigma \ge 0$. Taking derivatives with respect to $\sigma$:
\begin{equation}
    f'(\sigma) = \frac{(\sigma^2 + \lambda) - \sigma(2\sigma)}{(\sigma^2 + \lambda)^2} = \frac{\lambda - \sigma^2}{(\sigma^2 + \lambda)^2}.
\end{equation}
Equating $f'(\sigma) = 0$ gives the unique critical point $\sigma^* = \sqrt{\lambda}$. Evaluating $f(\sigma^*)$ as:
\begin{equation}
    f(\sqrt{\lambda}) = \frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{(\sqrt{\lambda})^2 + \lambda} = \frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{2\lambda} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}.
\end{equation}
As $f(0) = 0$ and $\lim_{\sigma \to \infty} f(\sigma) = 0$, then the unique global maximum on $[0, \infty)$ is $\sigma^* = \sqrt{\lambda}$. Thus, for any matrix $T$ with singular values $\{\sigma_i\}_{i=1}^K$, the spectral norm satisfies $\|T^{\dagger}_{\lambda}\|_2 = \max_i f(\sigma_i) \le \sup_{\sigma \ge 0} f(\sigma) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}$. For forward loss $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{risk}}$, chain rule yields $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{risk}} = (\nabla_{\tilde{\mathbf{p}}} \ell_{\mathrm{CE}}) T^T \nabla_\theta \mathbf{p}$. Row-stochasticity ensures $\|T\|_\infty = 1$ and $\|T\|_1 \le K$, and therefore, $\|T\|_2 \le \sqrt{K}$, completing the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Forward Surrogate Risk Consistency and Excess Clean Risk Bounds]
\label{thm:consistency}
Denote $\mathcal{R}^*_{\mathrm{CE}}(f) = \mathbb{E}_{(X, Y^*)} [\ell_{\mathrm{CE}}(f(X), Y^*)]$ to be the Bayes risk over clean distribution $P(X, Y^*)$, and denote $\hat{T}$ to be the estimated anchor transition matrix such that Frobenius estimation error $\|\hat{T} - T^*\|_F \le \delta$. The forward-corrected surrogate loss is $\ell_{\mathrm{forward}}(f(X), \tilde{Y}; \hat{T}) = -\log (\hat{T}^T f(X))_{\tilde{Y}}$, and empirical risk is $\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathrm{forward}}(f; \hat{T})$. Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:anchor} and~\ref{ass:bounded} with bounded logits and hypothesis Rademacher complexity $\mathfrak{R}_N(\mathcal{F}) \le \frac{C_{\mathcal{F}}}{\sqrt{N}}$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Minimizer Invariance}: Even though the difference between the surrogate forward risk and Bayes risk is the irreducible label transition entropy gap $\Delta_H \triangleq \mathbb{E}_X [H(T^{*T}\mathbf{p}^*(X)) - H(\mathbf{p}^*(X))] \ge 0$, their minimizers are identical to each other, i.e., $f^*(X) = \mathbf{p}^*(X) = P(Y^* \mid X)$.
    \item \textbf{Excess Clean Risk Consistency}: For empirical surrogate minimizer $\hat{f} = \arg\min_f \hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathrm{forward}}(f; \hat{T})$, the clean excess risk $\mathcal{E}^*(\hat{f}) = \mathcal{R}^*(\hat{f}) - \min \mathcal{R}^*(f)$ is such that:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{E}^*(\hat{f}) \le \frac{L_{\mathrm{CE}}}{\sigma_0} \delta + \mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right),
    \end{equation}
    where $L_{\mathrm{CE}} \le e^{2C}$ is the Lipschitz constant of the log-softmax mapping on bounded logits $[-C, C]$.
    \item \textbf{Confidence Inversion Bound}: The Tikhonov-regularized confidence inversion error for sample reliability filtering satisfies:
    \begin{equation}
        \|\hat{T}^{\dagger}_\lambda - (T^*)^{-1}\|_2 \le \frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\lambda}} + \frac{\lambda}{\sigma_0(\sigma_0^2 + \lambda)}.
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For Part 1, by the law of total expectation given $X$:
\begin{align}
    \mathbb{E}_{\tilde{Y}|X}&\left[\ell_{\mathrm{forward}}(f(X), \tilde{Y}; T^*)\right] \nonumber \\
    &= -\sum_{j=1}^K P(\tilde{Y}=j \mid X) \log (T^{*T} f(X))_j \nonumber \\
    &= H(T^{*T} \mathbf{p}^*(X)) + D_{\mathrm{KL}}(T^{*T} \mathbf{p}^*(X) \parallel T^{*T} f(X)),
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{p}^*(X) = P(Y^* \mid X)$. For clean Bayes risk:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}^*(f) = \mathbb{E}_X [H(\mathbf{p}^*(X)) + D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbf{p}^*(X) \parallel f(X))].
\end{equation}
Both attain minimum risk for zero KL divergence: $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(T^{*T} \mathbf{p}^* \parallel T^{*T} f) = 0 \iff T^{*T} f(X) = T^{*T} \mathbf{p}^*(X)$. Since $\sigma_{\min}(T^*) \ge \sigma_0 > 0$, then $T^{*T}$ is strictly invertible resulting in $f^*(X) = \mathbf{p}^*(X)$. The base risks differ only by a constant $\Delta_H$ independent of hypotheses.

For Part 2: For all $f$, clean excess risk is given by $\mathcal{E}^*(f) = \mathbb{E}_X [D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathbf{p}^*(X) \parallel f(X))]$. By Pinsker's inequality and non-singularity $\|T^{*T}\mathbf{v}\|_2 \ge \sigma_0 \|\mathbf{v}\|_2$:
\begin{equation}
    \|\mathbf{p}^*(X) - f(X)\|_1 \le \frac{1}{\sigma_0} \|T^{*T}(\mathbf{p}^*(X) - f(X))\|_1.
\end{equation}
By Rademacher generalization bounds on $\mathcal{F}$ with bounded loss, with probability at least $1 - \eta$:
\begin{equation}
    \sup_{f \in \mathcal{F}} |\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathrm{forward}}(f; \hat{T}) - \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{forward}}(f; T^*)| \le L_{\mathrm{CE}} \delta + \mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\right).
\end{equation}
Evaluating at empirical minimizer $\hat{f}$ yields $\mathcal{E}^*(\hat{f}) \le \frac{L_{\mathrm{CE}}}{\sigma_0} \delta + \mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{N})$, proving excess risk consistency.

For Part 3, decomposing regularized pseudo-inverse perturbation:
\begin{align}
    \|\hat{T}^{\dagger}_\lambda - (T^*)^{-1}\|_2 &\le \|\hat{T}^{\dagger}_\lambda - T^{*\dagger}_\lambda\|_2 + \|T^{*\dagger}_\lambda - (T^*)^{-1}\|_2 \nonumber \\
    &\le \frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\lambda}} + \frac{\lambda}{\sigma_0(\sigma_0^2 + \lambda)},
\end{align}
completing the proof.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Formal Mathematical Definitions of Evaluation Metrics}
To ensure rigorous reproducibility and unambiguous benchmarking:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Recall@K \& nDCG@K}: Standard top $K$ ranking accuracy metrics are evaluated against ground truth test interactions.
    \item \textbf{Raw Long-Tail Coverage (LTC@K)}: The fraction of unique tail catalog items that are recommended across all users:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{LTC@K} = \frac{|\bigcup_{u \in \mathcal{U}} \text{TopK}(u) \cap \mathcal{I}_{\text{tail}}|}{|\mathcal{I}_{\text{tail}}|}.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Genuine Long-Tail Coverage ($g$-LTC@K)}: Catalog coverage of authentic long tail items excluding injected attack targets:
    \begin{equation}
        g\text{-LTC@K} = \frac{|\bigcup_{u \in \mathcal{U}} \text{TopK}(u) \cap (\mathcal{I}_{\text{tail}} \setminus \mathcal{I}_{\text{target}})|}{|\mathcal{I}_{\text{tail}} \setminus \mathcal{I}_{\text{target}}|}.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Spam Target Exposure Rate (STER@K)}: Fraction of designated attack target items which are forced into user recommendation lists:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{STER@K} = \frac{|\bigcup_{u \in \mathcal{U}} \text{TopK}(u) \cap \mathcal{I}_{\text{target}}|}{|\mathcal{I}_{\text{target}}|}.
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Root Mean Square Error of Popularity Calibration (RMSE-PC)}:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{RMSE-PC} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|} \sum_{u \in \mathcal{U}} \left( \frac{|\text{TopK}(u) \cap \mathcal{I}_{\text{tail}}|}{K} - \alpha_u^* \right)^2},
    \end{equation}
    where $\alpha_u^*$ is user $u$'s true historical tail interaction ratio.
    \item \textbf{Mean Rank Miscalibration (MRMC)}:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{MRMC} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}|} \sum_{u \in \mathcal{U}} D_{\text{KL}}(\mathbf{p}_{\text{rec}}(u) \parallel \mathbf{p}_{\text{hist}}(u)),
    \end{equation}
    measuring KL divergence between recommended and historical popularity quintile distributions.
    \item \textbf{General Key Performance Indicator (GKPI)}: The harmonic mean of ranking utility, calibration fidelity, catalog coverage, and novelty:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{GKPI} = \frac{4}{\text{nDCG}^{-1} + (1 - \text{RMSE})^{-1} + \text{LTC}^{-1} + \frac{K_{\text{max}}}{\text{Nov}}}.
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\section{The RRFN-LLM-SUPER Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

The RRFN-LLM-SUPER architecture proceeds through five interconnected stages, detailed below:

\subsection{Stage 1: Anchor Selection \& Tikhonov Noise Inversion}
We consider anchor set $\mathcal{A}_{\text{clean}} \subset \mathcal{I}$, which satisfies Assumption~\ref{ass:anchor}. For each rating group $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$, row vector $T_{k,:}$ is estimated by consensus kernel density estimation. Loss function with risk-consistency regularization based on anchor prior $\hat{T}$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{risk}} = \frac{1}{\sum_i w_i} \sum_{i=1}^B w_i \cdot \ell_{\text{CE}}\left(T^T \mathbf{p}_i, \tilde{\mathbf{y}}_i\right) + \beta_{\text{reg}} \|T - \hat{T}\|_F^2.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stage 2: Multi-view Interaction Reliability Filter (MIRF)}
MIRF synthesizes three complementary signal views:

\textbf{1. Statistical Model Consistency ($R_{\text{RRFN}}$)}:
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{RRFN}}(u, i) = \exp\left(-\frac{|\tilde{r}_{ui} - \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{p}(u,i)}[Y^*]|^2}{2 \sigma_{\text{RRFN}}^2}\right).
\end{equation}

\textbf{2. LLM Semantic Review Text Auditing ($R_{\text{LLM}}$)}: When review text $S_{ui}$ is available, prompt auditing~\cite{bao2023tallrec} verifies sentiment alignment and bot phrasing:
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{LLM}}(u, i) = \text{LLM\_Audit}(S_{ui}, \tilde{r}_{ui}) \in [0, 1].
\end{equation}
Results are indexed in a local SQLite database configured with Write Ahead Logging (\texttt{PRAGMA journal\_mode=WAL}) for $\mathcal{O}(1)$ lookup.

\textbf{3. Vectorized Review Bombing Burst Detection ($R_{\text{bomb}}$)}:
\begin{equation}
    R_{\text{bomb}}(u, i) = 1 - \sigma\left(\omega_1 Z_{\text{temp}}(i, t) + \omega_2 S_{\text{pol}}(i, t) - \theta_{\text{bomb}}\right),
\end{equation}
Where $Z_{\text{temp}}$ represents rolling temporal acceleration and $S_{\text{pol}} = \frac{|N_1 - N_5|}{N_1 + N_5 + \epsilon}$ denotes polarity skew, calculated simultaneously by 2D NumPy array matrix multiplication ($W @ X$), observed to have $4.95\,$ms latency in 42K interactions ($>500$ times faster than nested Python loops).

\textbf{MIRF Signal Synthesis and Detection Metric Formulation}: The unified interaction reliability weight $w_{ui} = R_{\text{MIRF}}(u, i) \in [0, 1]$ is computed as:
\begin{equation}
    w_{ui} = \alpha R_{\text{RRFN}}(u, i) + \beta R_{\text{LLM}}(u, i) + \gamma R_{\text{bomb}}(u, i),
\end{equation}
where $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 1$. For evaluation of interaction reliability on graph edges:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Attack Detection Task}: Positive class ($y_{\text{attack}}=1$) refers to attack ratings. Anomaly confidence is measured by $s_{ui} = 1 - w_{ui}$. For Amazon ($\pi_{\text{edge}} = 17.40\%$), MIRF obtains $\text{AP}_{\text{attack}} = \mathbf{0.6884 \pm 0.0185}$ ($3.9\times$ higher than the $0.1748$ random baseline). For Yelp ($\pi_{\text{edge}} = 14.86\%$), MIRF obtains $\text{AP}_{\text{attack}} = \mathbf{0.6816 \pm 0.0239}$ (detector precision $\approx 0.6835$, achieving a $4.6\times$ lift over the $0.1486$ random baseline). For MovieLens-1M (899 attack edges out of 238,320 interactions, $\pi_{\text{edge}} = 0.38\%$), $\text{AP}_{\text{attack}} = \mathbf{0.0122 \pm 0.0021}$ ($3.2\times$ higher than random baseline $0.0038$).
    \item \textbf{Authentic Retention Task}: Evaluation of preserving authentic interactions ($y_{\text{auth}}=1$, score $w_{ui}$) results in $\text{AP}_{\text{auth}} = \mathbf{0.9989 \pm 0.0000}$ on MovieLens-1M (with Authentic Retention F1 = $\mathbf{0.9513 \pm 0.0010}$, Precision = $0.9225$, Recall = $0.9820$), $\mathbf{0.9295 \pm 0.0065}$ on Amazon, and $\mathbf{0.9491 \pm 0.0019}$ on Yelp.
    \item \textbf{Discrimination ROC-AUC}: Symmetric ranking discrimination gives $\mathbf{0.7931 \pm 0.0038}$ on MovieLens-1M, $\mathbf{0.7867 \pm 0.0208}$ on Amazon, and $\mathbf{0.7961 \pm 0.0232}$ on Yelp.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Stage 3: Graph Contrastive Learning \& Dual Models}
Let $\mathbf{e}_u, \mathbf{e}_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ denote graph embeddings. SimGCL views inject uniform noise $\mathbf{z}_i' = \mathbf{e}_i + \epsilon \cdot \text{sgn}(\mathbf{e}_i) \odot \Delta'$, while SGL views apply structural edge dropping $\hat{A}' = (M' \odot A) D'^{-1}$.

\textbf{Risk-Aware InfoNCE Loss}: Following contrastive representation principles~\cite{chen2020simple}, we formulate the sample-weighted alignment loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{cl}} = -\sum_{i \in \mathcal{B}} w_i \log \frac{\exp(\text{sim}(\mathbf{z}_i', \mathbf{z}_i'') / \tau)}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{B}} \exp(\text{sim}(\mathbf{z}_i', \mathbf{z}_j'') / \tau)}.
\end{equation}
Poisoned interactions ($w_i \to 0$) contribute zero pull force in contrastive alignment.

\textbf{Dual Partition Training}: Under Bayesian personalized ranking paradigms~\cite{rendle2009bpr}, the catalog is split according to $\rho_{\text{pareto}} = 0.20$ into $\mathcal{I}_{\text{head}}$ and $\mathcal{I}_{\text{tail}}$. The head model $M_{\text{pop}}$ is trained using $\mathcal{L}_{\text{risk}}^{\text{head}} + \lambda_{\text{cl}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{cl}}^{\text{head}}$, while the tail model $M_{\text{tail}}$ is trained using $\mathcal{L}_{\text{risk}}^{\text{tail}} + \lambda_{\text{cl}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{cl}}^{\text{tail}}$.

\subsection{Stage 4: Denoised SUPER Blueprint \& Rank-Calibrated Interleaving}
For user $u$, the denoised blueprint purges attack edges:
\begin{equation}
    B_u = \{i \in \mathcal{I} \mid (u, i) \in \mathcal{E} \land w_{ui} \ge \theta_{\text{trust}}\}.
\end{equation}
Personalized tail inclination is computed with normalized reliability weights:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_u = \frac{\sum_{i \in B_u \cap \mathcal{I}_{\text{tail}}} w_{ui}}{\sum_{i \in B_u} w_{ui} + \epsilon_{\text{smooth}}}.
\end{equation}
To minimize Mean Rank Miscalibration (MRMC), recommendations are assembled using rank calibrated trajectory interleaving, dynamically selecting between Head ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{head}} \setminus B_u$) and Tail ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{tail}} \setminus B_u$) candidate queues at each rank $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ to continuously track cumulative inclination $\alpha_u$.

\subsection{Experimental Setup, Datasets \& Multi-Seed Protocol}
Exact Dataset Specifications:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{MovieLens-1M benchmark sub-graph}: Derived from GroupLens MovieLens-1M~\cite{harper2015movielens} (1,000,209 ratings by 6,040 users of 3,900 movies). Processed using $k$-core iterative pruning ($k_{\text{user}} \ge 10, k_{\text{item}} \ge 5$). The sub-graph includes the most 300 active users and contains $|\mathcal{U}| = 300$ users, $|\mathcal{I}| = 3,405$ items (208 Head items, 3,197 Tail items in Pareto ratio $\rho = 0.20$) and 237,421 clean training interactions. Modalities: Discrete rating (1--5), timestamp, movie genres. No review text available; MIRF weights adjust dynamically to ($\alpha = 0.55, \beta = 0.00, \gamma = 0.45$).
    \item \textbf{Amazon Reviews (Electronics) sub-graph}~\cite{ni2019justifying}: 5-core filtered ecommerce sub-graph ($|\mathcal{U}| = 60$ users, $|\mathcal{I}| = 81$ items, 888 train interactions), constructed to act as a dense multi-modal prototype in order to test LLM review verification with controlled API caching. Tested using SimGCL backbone ($\lambda_{\text{cl}} = 0.15, \tau = 0.15$, 8 dual epochs on CUDA). Modalities: Ratings, timestamps, natural language review text ($\alpha = 0.40, \beta = 0.30, \gamma = 0.30$).
    \item \textbf{Yelp Academic sub-graph}: 5-core filtered local business sub-graph ($|\mathcal{U}| = 70$ users, $|\mathcal{I}| = 100$ items, 1,152 train interactions), tested using SGL backbone ($\lambda_{\text{cl}} = 0.15, \tau = 0.15$, 8 dual epochs on CUDA). Modalities: Ratings, timestamps, dense text reviews ($\alpha = 0.40, \beta = 0.30, \gamma = 0.30$).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Methodological Justification for Sub-Graph Benchmarking}: The multi-view MIRF framework integrates semantic natural language review auditing (via LLM evaluation and SQLite WAL caching) and 5-seed repeated trials ($S = \{42, 43, 44, 45, 46\}$) across all comparative baselines. Evaluating the multi-seed LLM auditing pipeline across full catalog scales ($>50,000$ reviews) would incur prohibitive sequential API latency and financial cost. Consequently, empirical benchmarks in Tables~\ref{tab:movielens_benchmark}--\ref{tab:ablation} are evaluated on these dense prototype sub-graphs ($|\mathcal{U}| \in [60, 300]$). Simultaneously, our data ingestion architecture (\texttt{loader.py}, \texttt{amazon\_loader.py}, \texttt{yelp\_loader.py}) is fully scaled and verified against standard full-scale benchmarks (MovieLens-1M: 6,040 users, 1,000,209 ratings; Amazon: 4,905 users, 53,258 interactions; Yelp: 1,200 businesses, 26,000 interactions), with automated verification in \texttt{test\_dataset\_scaling.py}.

\textbf{Data Partitioning and Strict Zero-Leakage Adversarial Attack Injection}: Data partitioning is performed strictly before adversarial attack injection using leave-one-out temporal order for each user $u \in \mathcal{U}$, where their last interaction is taken to the test set $\mathcal{I}_u^{\text{test}}$, second-last interaction to the validation set $\mathcal{I}_u^{\text{val}}$, and all other interactions to the training graph $\mathcal{G}_{\text{train}}$. Adversarial shilling attacks are injected only into the training graph $\mathcal{G}_{\text{train}}$ after temporal splitting. Validation and test sets remain strictly clean and untouched to ensure zero test set leakage or contamination with synthetic labels.

\textbf{Protocol for Adversarial Attacks Generation}: To assess the robustness of the defense against adversarial attacks, we consider three different types of adversarial campaigns:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bandwagon Shilling Campaigns ($\rho \in [0.05, 0.25]$)}: When $\rho = 0.15$, number of shills $N_{\text{shill}} = \lceil \rho |\mathcal{U}| \rceil$ is generated. Every shill profile generates 5-star ratings on the target tail items $\mathcal{I}_{\text{target}} \subset \mathcal{I}_{\text{tail}}$ in order to increase their exposure, 4- and 5-star ratings on the top popular anchor items $\mathcal{I}_{\text{push}} \subset \mathcal{I}_{\text{head}}$ to mask their profile, and randomly generated filler ratings from normal distribution $\mathcal{N}(\mu_r, \sigma_r^2)$.
    \item \textbf{Random Rating Noise ($\eta \in [0.05, 0.20]$)}: Uniformly distributed symmetric noise in ratings.
    \item \textbf{Review-Bombing Nuke Campaigns}: Coordinated negative rating bursts concentrated on competitor items within narrow time windows ($Z_{\text{temp}} > 2.5, S_{\text{pol}} > 0.70$).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Multi-Seed Repeated Evaluation Setup}: In order to achieve absolute empirical reproducibility and rigorous statistics, all the experiments on all three datasets and all backbones are conducted on $S = 5$ independent random seeds ($S = \{42, 43, 44, 45, 46\}$). We report empirical mean and standard deviation across all 5 trials ($\text{Mean} \pm \text{Std}$). Significance level is calculated by means of two-tailed paired Student's $t$-test (degrees of freedom $df = 4$) between RRFN-LLM-SUPER and the attacked model ($^*p < 0.05$).

\subsection{Main Benchmark Results \& Trade-Off Analysis}

\textbf{1. Popularity Calibration Superiority (RMSE-PC and MRMC)}: For all three datasets, there is a statistically significant gain in popularity calibration fidelity for attacked data over attacked baselines:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MovieLens-1M}: RMSE-PC improves from $0.1111 \pm 0.0044$ to $\mathbf{0.1067 \pm 0.0056}$ ($p = 0.0166^*$) and MRMC goes from $0.1159 \pm 0.0029$ to $\mathbf{0.1128 \pm 0.0034}$ ($p = 0.0088^*$).
    \item \textbf{Amazon (SimGCL)}: RMSE-PC goes from $0.1605 \pm 0.0068$ to $\mathbf{0.1459 \pm 0.0050}$ ($p = 0.0068^*$).
    \item \textbf{Yelp (SGL)}: RMSE-PC is reduced from $0.1236 \pm 0.0050$ to $\mathbf{0.1090 \pm 0.0004}$ ($p = 0.0026^*$), and MRMC decreases from $0.1164 \pm 0.0019$ to $\mathbf{0.1106 \pm 0.0013}$ ($p = 0.0006^*$).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{2. Purging Deceptive Tail Inflation vs. Genuine LTC}: Bandwagon attacks introduce the inflation of LTC@10 from $0.1428$ to $0.1575$ on MovieLens dataset, since bots generate huge volumes of interaction w.r.t. low degree items. Yet when calculating the Spam Target Exposure Rate (STER@10) metric, one can see that it was a deceiving improvement since 88\% of target items are exposed under Attacked Vanilla SUPER. RRFN-LLM-SUPER filters out corrupted interactions ($w_{ui} \to 0.02$) and therefore reduces spam exposure from $88.0\%$ to $72.0\%$ on MovieLens-1M and from $48.0\%$ to $40.0\%$ on Amazon.

Note that RRFN-LLM-SUPER significantly outperforms all baselines in terms of genuine long-tail coverage ($g$-LTC@10), which is the coverage of unattacked genuine tail items: Amazon $g$-LTC@10 goes from $0.4506 \pm 0.1275$ to $\mathbf{0.5155 \pm 0.0807}$ ($+14.4\%$), while Yelp $g$-LTC@10 goes from $0.3371 \pm 0.0693$ to $\mathbf{0.4202 \pm 0.0333}$ ($p = 0.0397^*$, $+24.7\%$ more genuine discoveries).

\textbf{3. Attack Denoising Detection Precision \& ROC Unified}: In order to avoid any possible confusion due to ambiguous metrics, we assess MIRF using two mutually complementary viewpoints of the edge attacks:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Attack detection ($\text{AP}_{\text{attack}}$)}: Evaluates the ability to detect minority injected attack ratings ($y = 1$ for attacks, measured by $1 - w_{ui}$). On Amazon ($\pi_{\text{edge}} = 17.40\%$), MIRF achieves $\text{AP}_{\text{attack}} = \mathbf{0.6884 \pm 0.0185}$, which is a $3.9\times$ improvement over the random baseline $\text{AP}_{\text{rand}} = 0.1748$. On Yelp ($\pi_{\text{edge}} = 14.86\%$), MIRF achieves $\text{AP}_{\text{attack}} = \mathbf{0.6816 \pm 0.0239}$ (detector precision $\approx 0.6835$, representing a $4.6\times$ lift over the $0.1486$ random baseline). On MovieLens-1M dataset (899 attack edges out of 238,320 total interactions, $\pi_{\text{edge}} = 0.38\%$), MIRF achieves $\text{AP}_{\text{attack}} = \mathbf{0.0122 \pm 0.0021}$ ($3.2\times$ above the random baseline $0.0038$).
    \item \textbf{Authentic Retention ($\text{AP}_{\text{auth}}$ and $\text{F1}_{\text{auth}}$)}: Measures preservation of authentic interactions ($y = 1$ for clean interactions; scored by $w_{ui}$). Results in $\text{AP}_{\text{auth}} = \mathbf{0.9989 \pm 0.0000}$ on MovieLens-1M (with Authentic Retention F1-score $\mathbf{0.9513 \pm 0.0010}$, Precision: $0.9225$, Recall: $0.9820$ with $w_{ui} > 0.50$ threshold), $\mathbf{0.9295 \pm 0.0065}$ on Amazon and $\mathbf{0.9491 \pm 0.0019}$ on Yelp.
    \item \textbf{ROC-AUC}: Symmetric ranking discrimination gives $\mathbf{0.7931 \pm 0.0038}$ on MovieLens-1M, $\mathbf{0.7867 \pm 0.0208}$ on Amazon and $\mathbf{0.7961 \pm 0.0232}$ on Yelp.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{4. Trade-Off on Pareto Front Between Utility \& Trustworthiness}: On Amazon and Yelp, the attacked Vanilla SUPER has greater nominal Recall@10 ($0.2967$ vs. $0.2600$ on Amazon) and greater nominal nDCG@10 ($0.1326$ vs. $0.1051$). This is due to a deliberate Pareto trade-off of utility and catalog calibration against the accuracy of the model on the contaminated test dataset. In bandwagon shilling attacks, bots add high ratings on popular head filler items to disguise themselves; denoising is not performed by un-denoised collaborative filtering, thereby amplifying the popularity bias for popular head items, increasing top-$K$ precision on popular test sets. By design, RRFN-LLM-SUPER gives lower weights to these interactions ($w_i \to 0.02$), thereby shrinking the recommendation power on the compromised head items, and making room for genuine long tail item exploration ($g$-LTC@10 increased by $+24.7\%$ on Yelp, $p = 0.0397$).

\subsection{Ablation Analysis}
In Table~\ref{tab:ablation}, we report the systematic ablation study evaluating the 6 canonical variants on MovieLens-1M under 15\% bandwagon attack, demonstrating the strictly monotonic contribution of each framework module:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,parsep=0pt,topsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Full Model Superiority}: The unified RRFN-LLM-SUPER achieves optimal multi-metric performance (Recall@10 = $0.2333$, nDCG@10 = $0.1069$, LTC@10 = $0.1531$, ROC-AUC = $0.7028$), harmonizing accurate ranking with aggressive shilling defense and long-tail exposure.
    \item \textbf{w/o RRFN (No Transition Matrix $T$)}: Omitting the regularized transition matrix inversion eliminates risk-consistent loss correction; Denoising ROC-AUC collapses from $0.7028$ to $0.4416$ (a $-37.2\%$ drop), confirming that $T$-matrix inversion is essential to prevent representation corruption from shifted rating distributions.
    \item \textbf{w/o Bombing Detector ($\gamma = 0$)}: Removing temporal acceleration and polarity skew burst scoring causes the steepest detection failure, reducing Denoising ROC-AUC to $0.3953$ (a $-43.8\%$ drop).
    \item \textbf{w/o LLM Auditing ($\beta = 0$)}: Disabling semantic review validation leads to a drop in Denoising ROC-AUC to $0.6515$ ($-7.3\%$), showing the importance of semantic sentiment grounding.
    \item \textbf{w/o GCL Contrastive Loss ($\lambda_{\text{cl}} = 0$)}: Disabling self-supervised contrastive representation alignment reduces tail item discovery (LTC@10 drops to $0.1492$).
    \item \textbf{w/o SUPER Debiasing (Monolithic)}: Collapsing the decoupled dual head/tail partition back to a monolithic architecture induces a catastrophic $-44.5\%$ drop in tail coverage (LTC@10 plummets from $0.1531$ to $0.0849$) and degrades utility (Recall@10 drops to $0.1200$ and nDCG@10 to $0.0492$), as head items monopolize recommendation slots.
\end{enumerate}

Baseline values include CTR ($2.5\% \pm 0.5\%$), CVR ($3.0\% \pm 0.5\%$), and AOV ($\$35 \pm \$10$ for Amazon Electronics, $\$25 \pm \$5$ for Yelp services). \section{Discussion, Reproducibility \& Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Open-Source Reproducibility}: Complete source code, models, benchmark scripts, and evaluation logs are publicly accessible at: \url{https://github.com/advaitvarhade/RRFN-LLM-SUPER}. Deterministic reproduction steps: (1) benchmark: \texttt{python run\_multiseed\_benchmark.py}; (2) figures: \texttt{python paper/generate\_figures.py}; (3) tests: \texttt{pytest robust\_super/tests/ -v}.

\textbf{Vectorization Efficiency}: Using 2D BLAS matrix broadcasting, MIRF scores 42,000 user-item pairs in $4.95\,$ms ($>500\times$ faster than naive Python loops).

\textbf{Scalability \& Sub-Graph Prototyping}: While empirical 5-seed multi-view benchmarks in Tables~\ref{tab:movielens_benchmark}--\ref{tab:ablation} evaluate dense prototype sub-graphs (300 users on MovieLens, 60--70 users on Amazon/Yelp) to maintain feasible LLM API auditing latency across repeated trials, our underlying data architecture and vectorization engine support full-scale ingestion (MovieLens-1M: 1,000,209 edges across 6,040 users; Amazon: 53,258 edges; Yelp: 26,000 edges). For production deployments with millions of catalog items, asynchronous batching, hierarchical embedding retrieval, and offline BLAS vectorization prevent LLM inference bottlenecks.

\textbf{Cold Start Limitations}: In ultra-sparse regimes lacking clean anchor interactions, noise transition estimation defaults gracefully to category priors.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced RRFN-LLM-SUPER, a unified framework resolving both adversarial shilling vulnerability and catalog popularity bias. Theoretically, Tikhonov regularization guarantees operator boundedness ($\|T^{\dagger}_\lambda\|_2 \le \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}$) and excess risk consistency $\mathcal{O}(\delta/\sigma_0 + 1/\sqrt{N})$. Across 5-seed benchmarks on MovieLens-1M, Amazon, and Yelp, RRFN-LLM-SUPER achieves Attack Detection AP of 0.6884 and 0.6816 ($3.9$--$4.6\times$ over random baselines), Authentic Retention AP of 0.9989 with ROC-AUC = 0.7931, statistically significant reductions in popularity calibration errors (RMSE-PC, MRMC, $p < 0.05$), purges deceptive tail inflation (STER@10 reduced from 88\% to 72\%), and enhances genuine long-tail discovery ($g$-LTC@10 $+24.7\%$ on Yelp) with $>500\times$ vectorization acceleration.
